About me.

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science and Engineering at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, advised by Prof. Qiang Xu. I am also a research intern in the Time Series Foundation Model team at Ant International.

My research centers on Foundation Models for the Future: Forecasting, Evolving, and Science Discovering. I ask how foundation models can anticipate changes that have not yet happened, acquire abilities they do not yet have, and help discover laws we do not yet understand.

I work on time-series foundation models and their scaling laws, multimodal, probabilistic and agentic forecasting, and predictive world models. I also explore the reliability, memory and evolution of large language models, and their role in scientific discovery.

My work includes seven first-author papers, four accepted at CCF-A conferences or SCI Q1 journals, and three technical reports. I have also co-authored one CCF-A paper as a shared first author and five other CCF-A papers. My publications have received over 180 citations on Google Scholar.

03 / EXPERIENCE

Research experience.

Jan 2026 — PresentResearch Intern

Time Series Foundation Model Team

Background. Foundation models and LLM agents for forecasting and optimization in FX trading and liquidity management. The team covers over US$100 billion in trading and US$1.4 trillion in cash-flow management.

My role. Lead and co-lead work on foundation models, online evolution and forecasting agents, and participate in foundation-model development.

  • Online evolution. Proposed ORCA, which uses the context of forecast errors for online adaptation, improving performance by 6.3% on average across five time-series foundation models.
  • Time-series foundation models. Proposed a unified scaling law relating model capacity, historical information and forecast horizons, supporting the development of time-series foundation models. Participated in the development of Falcon-X for heterogeneous multivariate time series.
  • World models. Led Pythia, a multimodal predictive world model based on JEPA with Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B as its text encoder. On MUSE, it improves performance by 6.26% over the strongest of 31 published baselines. Released as a technical report.
  • Self-evolving forecasting agents. Co-led TimEvolve, where an LLM proposes candidate execution plans involving tool selection, evidence interpretation and forecast correction. As actual outcomes arrive, the agent evaluates the candidates and updates the control policy of its harness. It achieves the best average performance ranking among 15 methods across eight domains.

Outcomes. Two technical reports, including one as first author; two time-series foundation-model papers accepted at NeurIPS 2026, including one as first author.
